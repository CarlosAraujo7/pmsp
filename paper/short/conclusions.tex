\section{Conclusions}
\label{sec:conclusions}

This article asked what a learned policy adds to the metaheuristic that hosts it, and answered with a budget-fair attribution protocol applied to three scheduling problems and seven environments. The gain of Q-Learning-guided Iterated Greedy
comes from the search structure and the budget: the policy adds $0.01$--$0.05$ points for identical machines, about $0.1\%$ for unrelated machines and nothing for the flowshop; the active ingredient is
destruction--reconstruction, its combination with a descent, or the local search, depending on the problem; the verdict on a local search at equal CPU time depends on the efficiency of its implementation (an advantage of $0.15\%$ at equal iterations is $0.10\%$ at equal CPU when vectorized, $0.11\%$ when compiled, and $0.01\%$ in a first, loop-based version; a count of evaluations gives $0.08\%$ and depends on its cost model). A configuration chosen offline beats
the online agent in every environment (a validated destruction size or strategy in seven, a uniform mixture of the operators of an ILS in the eighth), against eleven learners (six of classes other than tabular or multi-armed, up to a DQN with replay and a target network and a policy-gradient learner, in spaces of up to $30$ actions; the best, a selector trained offline on $700$ instances, only matches it), and the headroom of the actions is necessary but not sufficient for a policy gain: the law that held for destruction strategies failed prospectively for the destruction size.
The learning-free solvers that follow are provably optimal on $97.5\%$ of the synthetic identical-machine instances and within $0.25$--$0.30\%$ of the best known solutions of the standard unrelated-machine benchmark.

Future work should (i) test richer action spaces, learners and rewards that arrive early enough in the run, and tighter budgets, where a policy might have room, with pre-specified action spaces of controlled headroom; (ii) apply the
protocol to other search frameworks and to problems where exact methods do not reach, such as heterogeneous machines with setup times; and (iii) pair the LP start and the restricted MILP with the stronger exact components of the literature.
The code, the frozen hashes, the dated record of every hypothesis (including the non-confirmed) and the audits that recompute the numbers of the text from the result files make the study auditable end to end.
