Search-phase analysis (f3 = progress):
  Early iterations (f3 in [0,40%]): dominant operator = inter-machine job swap (Op1) (89% of states in this phase)
  Late  iterations (f3 in [60,100%]): dominant operator = inter-machine job swap (Op1) (89% of states in this phase)
  --> The agent consistently prefers the same operator regardless of progress,
      indicating that this operator dominates across all search phases.

Stagnation analysis (f4 = fraction of iterations without improvement):
  Low  stagnation (f4 in [0,30%]): dominant operator = inter-machine job swap (Op1) (93%)
  High stagnation (f4 > 60%):        dominant operator = inter-machine job swap (Op1) (85%)
  --> Stagnation level does not change the preferred operator,
      suggesting the agent relies on perturbation (ILS kick) for escape.

Solution quality analysis (f1 = relative gap to lower bound):
  High gap (f1 > 60%): dominant operator = inter-machine job swap (Op1) (89%)
  Low  gap (f1 < 20%): dominant operator = inter-machine job swap (Op1) (89%)
  --> The preferred operator is invariant to solution quality.
