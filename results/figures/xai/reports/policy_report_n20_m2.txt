=== Policy Report: n=20, m=2 ===

Operator usage (fraction of states where it is greedy-optimal):
  Op1 (inter-swap)                 96.5%
  Op2 (reinsert)                    1.3%
  Op3 (intra-swap)                  0.0%
  Op4 (block-move)                  2.2%

Search-phase analysis (f3 = progress):
  Early iterations (f3 in [0,40%]): dominant operator = inter-machine job swap (Op1) (97% of states in this phase)
  Late  iterations (f3 in [60,100%]): dominant operator = inter-machine job swap (Op1) (96% of states in this phase)
  --> The agent consistently prefers the same operator regardless of progress,
      indicating that this operator dominates across all search phases.

Stagnation analysis (f4 = fraction of iterations without improvement):
  Low  stagnation (f4 in [0,30%]): dominant operator = inter-machine job swap (Op1) (95%)
  High stagnation (f4 > 60%):        dominant operator = inter-machine job swap (Op1) (99%)
  --> Stagnation level does not change the preferred operator,
      suggesting the agent relies on perturbation (ILS kick) for escape.

Solution quality analysis (f1 = relative gap to lower bound):
  High gap (f1 > 60%): dominant operator = inter-machine job swap (Op1) (97%)
  Low  gap (f1 < 20%): dominant operator = inter-machine job swap (Op1) (96%)
  --> The preferred operator is invariant to solution quality.

  Early search, unbalanced (f1=4,f2=4): inter-machine job swap (Op1) (84% of slice states)
  Early search, balanced   (f1=4,f2=0): inter-machine job swap (Op1) (100%)

Summary paragraph (for paper):
